\section{Solving Identification via the Mediator MTE}
\label{sec:selectionmodel}
If your goal is to estimate CM effects, and you could control for unobserved selection terms $\vec U_i$, then you would.
This ideal (but infeasible) scenario would yield unbiased estimates for the ADE and AIE.
Identification via the mediator Marginal Treatment Effect (MTE) takes this insight seriously, providing conditions to model the implied confounding and thus avoiding it.

The main problem is that second-stage regression equation \eqref{eqn:parametric-secondstage} is not identified, because $\vec U_i$ is unobserved, and leads to omitted variables bias.
\begin{align}
    \Egiven{Y_i}{Z_i, D_i, \vec X_i} \; =& \;
        \alpha
        + \beta D_i
        + \gamma Z_i
        + \delta Z_i D_i
        + \varphi(\vec X_i)
        \label{eqn:secondstage-reg} \\
        & \;\; +\underbrace{\left( 1 - D_i
            \right) \Egiven{ U_i(Z_i, 0) }{D_i(Z_i) = 0, \vec X_i}
                + D_i \Egiven{ U_i(Z_i, 1) }{D_i(Z_i) = 1, \vec X_i}}_{
                    \text{Unobserved confounding.}}
        \nonumber
\end{align}

My approach to identifying CM effects models unobserved selection-into-$D_i$ via the mediator MTE, avoiding the bias terms derived in \autoref{sec:mediation}.
Thinking on MTEs began with corrections for sample selection problems \citep{heckman1974shadow}, and were extended to a switching/selection problem in parametric settings \citep{heckman1979sample,bjorklund1987estimation} and a general case \citep{heckman2005structural}.
The approach works in the following manner: (1) assume that the variable of interest follows a threshold-crossing model, where unexplained first-stage selection informs unobserved second-stage confounding; (2) extract information about unobserved confounding from the first-stage using an instrumental variable; and (3) identify the ADE and AIE by interpolating the Mediator MTE.

Identification in MTE methods typically relies on identifying the corresponding Control Functions (CFs) with an external instrument or distributional assumptions; this paper focuses exclusively on the case that an instrument is available.
By explicitly accounting for the information contained in the first-stage selection model, the MTE approach enables consistent estimation of causal effects in the second-stage even when selection is driven by unobserved factors.

In the OHIE application, the MTE approach allows unobserved determinants of healthcare use to be associated with potential health outcomes. Instrument-induced variation in healthcare use then reveals how the causal effect of healthcare varies along this latent selection margin.
Then it uses this information in the second-stage estimation of how much the effect goes through increased healthcare usage, estimating the ADE and AIE after controlling for this confounding.

\subsection{MTE Model Set-up}
The mediator MTE measures how the average causal effect of mediator $D_i$ varies across the population distribution, measured by a uniform random variable $U_i$.
\begin{align*}
    \operatorname{MTE}(p; z, \vec X_i)
    &= \Egiven{Y_i(z, 1) - Y_i(z, 0)}{\vec X_i, U_i = p}\\
    &= \beta + \delta z + \Egiven{U_i(z, 1) - U_i(z, 0)}{\vec X_i, U_i = p},
    \;\;\;\; \text{ for } z = 0,1,\;p \in (0,1).
\end{align*}
Under the threshold-crossing selection model, a marginal increase in the propensity score induces mediator take-up among individuals whose latent mediator take-up cost $U_i$ is locally equal to $p$.

The following assumptions interpolate the mediator MTE across the relevant support, and thus both the ADE and AIE.

\theoremstyle{definition}
\newtheorem{assumptionMTE}{Assumption}
\renewcommand\theassumptionMTE{MTE--\arabic{assumptionMTE}}
\begin{assumptionMTE}
    \label{mte:monotonicity}
    Mediator monotonicity.
    \[ \Prob{ D_i(0) \leq D_i(1) } = 1. \]
\end{assumptionMTE}
Assumption \ref{mte:monotonicity} is the monotonicity condition first used in an IV context \citep{imbens1994identification}.
Here, it is assuming that people respond to treatment, $Z_i$, by consistently taking or refusing the mediator $D_i$ (always or never-mediators), or taking the mediator $D_i$ if and only if assigned to the treatment $Z_i=1$ (mediator compliers).
There are no mediator defiers, so that the mediator switcher group ($D_i(0) \neq D_i(1)$) is comprised entirely of mediator compliers ($D_i(0) =0$, $D_i(1)=1$).

The main implication of Assumption \ref{mte:monotonicity} is that selection-into-mediator can be written as a selection model with ordered threshold crossing values that describe selection-into-$D_i$ \citep{vytlacil2002independence}.
\[ D_i(z) = \indicator{V_i \leq \psi \big( z; \vec X_i \big)},
    \;\text{ for } z=0,1 \]
where $V_i$ is a latent variable with continuous distribution and conditional cumulative density function $F_V(. \,|\vec X_i)$, and $\psi(. \,;\vec X_i)$ collects observed sources of mediator selection.
$V_i$ could be assumed to follow a known distribution; the canonical Heckman selection model assumes $V_i$ is normally distributed (a ``Heckit'' model).
The identification strategy here applies to the general case that the distribution of $V_i$ is unknown, without parametric restrictions.

I focus on the equivalent transformed model of \cite{heckman2005structural},
\[ D_i(z) = \indicator{U_i \leq \pi(z; \vec X_i)},
    \;\;\; \text{for } z=0,1 \]
where $U_i \coloneqq F_V\left( V_i \mid \vec X_i \right)$
follows a uniform distribution, and $\pi(z; \vec X_i) = F_V\big(\psi(z; \vec X_i)\big) = \Probgiven{D_i = 1}{Z_i = z, \vec X_i}$ is the mediator propensity score.
$U_i$ are the unobserved mediator take-up costs.
Note the maintained assumption that treatment $Z_i$ is quasi-randomly assigned conditional on $\vec X_i$ implies $Z_i \indep U_i$ conditional on $\vec X_i$.

This selection model setup is equivalent to the monotonicity condition, and is importing a well-known equivalence result from the IV literature to the CM setting.
The main conceptual difference is not assuming $Z_i$ is a valid instrument for identifying the $D_i$ on $Y_i$ effect among compliers; it is using the selection model representation to correct for selection bias.
See \appendixref{appendix:mte-monotonicity} for a validation of the general \cite{vytlacil2002independence} equivalence result in a CM setting, with conditioning covariates $\vec X_i$.

For notation purposes, suppose the vector of control variables $\vec X_i$ has at least two entries;
denote $\vec X_i^{\text{IV}}$ as one entry in the vector, and $\vec X_i^-$ as the remaining.
For each $z = 0,1$ and each possible value of $\vec x_i^{\mathrm{IV}}$ in the support of $\vec X_i^{\mathrm{IV}}$, write the mediator propensity score as
\[ \pi\big( z; \;\vec x^{\mathrm{IV}}, \vec x^- \big) = \Prob{D_i=1 \mid Z_i=z, \vec X_i^{\mathrm{IV}}=\vec x^{\mathrm{IV}}, \vec X_i^-=\vec x^-}. \]
Write $P_i = \pi(Z_i; \vec X_i^-, \vec X_i^{\mathrm{IV}})$ for the observed propensity score, and
$\mathcal P_{z} \left(\vec X_i^- \right) \coloneqq \operatorname{Supp}\left( P_i \mid z,\vec X_i^- \right)$ for the IV-guided propensity score support for each treatment arm $z=0,1$.
\begin{assumptionMTE}
    \label{mte:instrument}
    Mediator take-up cost instrument: $\vec X_i^{\mathrm{IV}}$ satisifes \emph{relevance}, \emph{exclusion}, \emph{quasi-random assignment}, and \emph{complier support}.
    
    The first three are standard IV assumptions, and the last assumes for $z=0,1$ and each $(\vec x^{\mathrm{IV}},\vec x^-)$ in the relevant support,
    \[ \Big[
        \pi(0;\vec x^{\mathrm{IV}},\vec x^-), \; \pi(1;\vec x^{\mathrm{IV}},\vec x^-) \Big]
    \subseteq \operatorname{Supp}\left( P_i \mid Z_i=z,\vec X_i^-=\vec x^- \right). \]
\end{assumptionMTE}
Assumption \ref{mte:instrument} is requiring at least one control variable guides selection-into-$D_i$ --- an Instrumental Variable (IV).
Relevance provides variation in the mediator propensity score.
Exclusion requires preceding healthcare access location to affect subsequent health and well-being only through healthcare take-up, after conditioning on $\vec X_i^-$ and $Z_i$.
Quasi-random assignment requires this variation to be unrelated to latent mediator choices and mediator potential outcomes after conditioning on $\vec X_i^-$.
Complier support requires variation in the mediator instrument, within each treatment arm and holding $\vec X_i^-$ fixed, to generate the propensity-score values associated with mediator take-up under both $Z_i=0$ and $Z_i=1$.
It therefore permits the conditional outcome function within the $Z_i=z$ arm to be evaluated at both endpoints of the mediator-complier interval.
\appendixref{appendix:mte-iv} gives these assumptions in full technical detail.

The exclusion and assignment conditions are substantive assumptions that must be defended using institutional knowledge.
The relevance condition has empirical implications that can be evaluated using the mediator first stage.
The most compelling example of a mediator IV is using data on the cost of mediator take-up as a first-stage IV, if it varies between individuals for unrelated reasons and is strong in explaining mediator take-up.
Complier support can be tested empirically among observed data, within each treatment value. 

\begin{assumptionMTE}
    \label{mte:joint-monotonicity}
    Common mediator-selection index.
    
    Treatment $Z_i$ and the mediator instrument $\vec X_i^{\mathrm{IV}}$ shift mediator take-up through the same scalar latent index $U_i$:
    \[ D_i(z,\vec x^{\mathrm{IV}}) = \indicator{ U_i \leq \pi(z;\vec x^{\mathrm{IV}},\vec X_i^-) }, \]
    for $z=0,1$ and $\vec x^{\mathrm{IV}}$ on the $\vec X_i^{\mathrm{IV}}$ support, almost surely conditional on $\vec X_i^-$.
\end{assumptionMTE}
This assumption requires changes in $Z_i$ and $\vec X_i^{\mathrm{IV}}$ to move the threshold on the same mediator-selection index.
The mediator compliers induced by treatment therefore lie on the same latent margin traced by variation in the mediator instrument.
Two-way monotonicity---monotonicity of mediator take-up in both treatment $Z_i$ and the mediator instrument $\vec X_i^{\mathrm{IV}}$---is sufficient for representing their effects on mediator take-up through a common scalar resistance index $U_i$.
In the OHIE, the assumption requires lottery selection and preceding healthcare access location to shift healthcare use along the same ordering of individuals by latent resistance to healthcare use.

For notation purposes, let $K_z$ denote the number of distinct values taken by the mediator propensity score $\pi(z;\; \vec X_i^{\mathrm{IV}}, \vec X_i^-)$, for each $z=0,1$.
\theoremstyle{definition}
\newtheorem{assumptionMTECF}{Assumption}
\renewcommand\theassumptionMTECF{MTE--CF}
\begin{assumptionMTECF}
    \label{mte:restricted-cf}
    Restricted and separable Control Functions (CFs).
    The CFs belong to a $K_z - 1$ dimensional class over the complier distribution, and are separable from control variables,
    for $z=0,1$ and $p \in (0,1)$
    \begin{align*}
        \Egiven{U_i(z, 0)}{p<U_i, \vec X_i^-}
        &= \underbrace{\Egiven{U_i(z,0)}{p<U_i}}_{\eqqcolon g_{z,0}(p)}
        + \vec \phi_{z,0}' \vec X_i^-, \\
        \Egiven{U_i(z,1)}{U_i\leq p, \vec X_i^-}
        &= \underbrace{\Egiven{U_i(z,1)}{U_i\leq p}}_{\eqqcolon g_{z,1}(p)}
        + \vec \phi_{z,1}' \vec X_i^-.
    \end{align*}
\end{assumptionMTECF}

After observed heterogeneity is absorbed by $\mu_d(z;\vec X_i)$, then within each $Z_i = z$ remaining dependence between observed mediator take-up cost $U_i$ and outcome gains $U_i(z,0), U_i(z,1)$ is common across $\vec X_i^-$ and lies in a $K_z-1$ dimensional basis in the complier region, $\pi(0;\; \vec X_i^{\mathrm{IV}}, \vec X_i^-)$ to $\pi(1; \;\vec X_i^{\mathrm{IV}}, \vec X_i^-)$.
Equivalently, the instrument identifies the CFs only up to the degrees of freedom supplied by variation in the instrument.
For example, with only two relevant propensity-score values, this is a linear restriction on the CFs, corresponding to the restricted MTE logic of \cite{brinch2017beyond}.
This restricted support assumption is weaker than relying on identification at infinity, with testable implications --- described further in \appendixref{appendix:mte-restricted}.

\subsection{Re-identification of CM Effects}
This section uses the above assumptions to identify the ADE and AIE in observed data, after using appropriate variation in mediator take-up supplied by the appropriate IV.

Write $m\big(p; z, \vec X_i^-\big) = \Egiven{Y_i}{Z_i = z, \vec X_i^-, P_i = p}$, the mean outcome given treatment, control values, and observed propensity score $P_i$.
Assume it is finite for all values, and is absolutely continuous and its derivative exists almost everywhere.
\begin{proposition}
    \label{proposition:secondstage}
    If assumptions \ref{mte:monotonicity}, \ref{mte:instrument} \ref{mte:joint-monotonicity}, and \ref{mte:restricted-cf} hold, then the outcome mean conditional on the propensity score is identified in observed data
    \[ m\big(p; z, \vec X_i^-\big)
    = \alpha + \gamma z + p \big(\beta + \delta z)
    + \phi^m(\vec X_i^-)
    + (1-p) g_{z,0}(p)
    + p g_{z,1}(p), \]
    for each $p \in \mathcal P_{z} \left(\vec X_i^- \right)$ and $z=0,1$.
    $\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta$ are the same parameters as in \autoref{sec:applied}, and $g_{z,0}, g_{z,1}$ are the CFs defined above (with an assumed restricted shape).
    Furthermore, the average Mediator MTE is point-wise identified on the propensity score support, as the difference between conditional means
    \[ m\big(p'; z, \vec X_i^-\big) - m\big(p\,; z, \vec X_i^-\big)
    = \int_p^{p'} \operatorname{MTE}(u; z, \vec X_i^-) \,du, \]
    for any $p<p'$ such that
    $\big[ p,p' \big] \subseteq \mathcal P_z(\vec X_i^-)$, and $z = 0,1$.
    Proof: see \appendixref{appendix:mte-ade-aie}.
\end{proposition}

The mean difference of the mediator MTE is identified under the \ref{thm:mte-identification} assumptions, thanks to instrumental variation in $\vec X_i^{\text{IV}}$.
The final step interpolates from the Mediator MTE to mediator compliers, and thus identifies the ADE and AIE.

\newtheorem{theoremMTE}{Theorem}
\renewcommand\thetheoremMTE{MTE Approach}
\begin{theoremMTE}
    \label{thm:mte-identification}
    If assumptions \ref{mte:monotonicity}, \ref{mte:instrument} \ref{mte:joint-monotonicity}, and \ref{mte:restricted-cf} hold, then the ADE and AIE are identified as a function of the parameters in Proposition \ref{proposition:secondstage}.
    \begin{align*}
        \text{AIE}(z) &= \E{
            m\big(\pi(1; \vec X_i^-, \vec X_i^\text{IV})\,; z, \vec X_i^- \big)
            - m\big(\pi(0; \vec X_i^-, \vec X_i^\text{IV})\,; z, \vec X_i^- \big)
            } \\
        \text{ADE}(1-z) &=
            \text{ATE} - \text{AIE}(z), \;\;\;\; \text{ for each } z=0,1.
    \end{align*}
    Proof: see \appendixref{appendix:mte-ade-aie}.
\end{theoremMTE}

This theorem provides a solution to the identification problem for CM effects when facing selection.
The MTE approach measures both selection bias and complier differences, and thus purges these persistent bias terms to identify CM effects.
